### Topics Discussed & Decisions

**1. McGill interval holds as the default for ALL strength holds**
- Douglas loves the McGill short-hold restructure on side plank and asked: why not use it for all timed holds?
- Agreed: the principle is general, not side-plank-specific. Core stability is an endurance quality; short repeated holds keep form crisp and kill the grind.
- **Reframed as a ceiling rule:** set a max single-hold duration (~30–40s for core holds). Below it = one continuous hold; once progression would exceed it, add a *rep* instead of seconds. Self-applies over time, no per-exercise decision needed.
- **Exception:** stretching/mobility holds stay continuous (time-under-stretch is the point).
- Priority convert: **Plank** (currently ~75s/set, the one that grinds). Hollow body also flagged — intervals are a *safety* win there (back stays on floor).

**2. Stretching set revisions (verified against sources)**
- Guiding principle established: **stretch a muscle by moving opposite to its action.**
- **Lower back day → child's pose, lying spinal twist, figure-four.** Dropped cobra (it's *extension* — contracts the erectors you just worked) and cat-cow (low-value mobility drill).
- **Abs day → cobra, lying spinal twist, side-bend.** Cobra is *correct* here (extension releases the flexed abs).
- **Glutes day → figure-four/pigeon, lying spinal twist, hip flexor stretch.** Hip flexor kept deliberately despite being the antagonist — Douglas sits ~8h/day, so it's his highest-value mobility work.

**3. Lower back exercises — the thin-library wall**
- Douglas raised three: bodyweight **good mornings** (too light), **bird dog at 28 reps/side** (no longer challenging), **reverse hyper** (room can't accommodate it).
- Verdicts: good morning = needs load (band it) or retire; **bird dog = retire** (it's a motor-control drill, skill mastered, more reps just makes it endurance); **reverse hyper = remove** (impractical), floor substitute is reverse superman / leg-lift.
- Key insight: the lower back is a **posterior-chain** muscle, hard to progressively overload in isolation with bodyweight. It's best trained via hip hinges (which also train glutes + hams).

**4. KEY DECISION — Fold lower back into a "Posterior-Chain Day"**
- Douglas's goal for lower back = **maintain, not build** (the original mission — kill the pain — is accomplished).
- He questioned whether a dedicated lower-back day is even needed, since erectors are already trained on glutes day (hinges = loaded) and abs day (planks = stabilized). Coach confirmed his instinct is sound — and noted that for back *health*, dynamic spinal extension is actually the *least* important stimulus (McGill favors stability over isolation extension).
- **Decision: drop the dedicated lower-back isolation day; fold the lower back into glutes day as a "Posterior-Chain Day."** Safety condition: that day must always include (a) a hip hinge and (b) one direct extension move as insurance.
- **Payoff:** this frees the 3rd rotation slot for a new muscle group (the headline item) → rotation becomes **Abs → Posterior Chain → [3rd slot]**.

**5. Posterior-Chain Day template (PROPOSED — pending Douglas's confirmation next session)**
- 3 slots filled by *movement role*, not random draw:
  - Slot 1 — **Hinge** (every session): single-leg RDL banded, banded good morning, band pull-through (weighted if kettlebell acquired)
  - Slot 2 — **Glute builder** (every session): hip thrust, glute bridge, single-leg glute bridge, frog pumps, banded glute bridge
  - Slot 3 — **Rotating accessory**: alternates between (a) erector extension (superman, reverse superman, back-ext hold, swimmers) and (b) glute-med/lateral (clamshells, fire hydrants, lateral band walk, donkey kicks, curtsy lunge)

**6. Equipment note**
- A single **kettlebell or dumbbells (~300–500 SEK)** became more compelling: on posterior-chain day a kettlebell loads glutes + hams + erectors in one hinge. Still optional (bands + bodyweight maintain). Pull-up bar still pending (depends on 3rd-slot decision + doorframe check).
